# Measured baseline — 2026-08-07

Step 1 of the rebuild programme (`REBUILD_STEPS.md`). This is the **measured**
state of the shipped curated artifacts on 2026-08-07. It supersedes the prose
snapshots in `CLAUDE.md`, which are dated and drift every daily run.

Every number below was measured in this run against a frozen snapshot, not
inherited from any earlier document. Where a figure in `REBUILD_PLAN.md` or
`REBUILD_STEPS.md` did **not** reproduce, that is stated plainly in
§ 8 rather than papered over.

## How to reproduce

The baseline is a content-hashed copy of `data/curated/`, frozen so later steps
have a fixed thing to diff against:

```bash
cd backend
.venv/bin/python scripts/snapshot_curated.py \
    --out ../data/baseline/2026-08-07 \
    --manifest-copy ../docs/baselines/MANIFEST_2026-08-07.json
```

`data/baseline/` is gitignored (~18 MB per vintage). The committed durable
record is `docs/baselines/MANIFEST_2026-08-07.json` — sha256, byte size, row
count, full column list, dtypes, and date ranges per file.

Diffing any later state against it:

```bash
.venv/bin/python scripts/diff_curated.py \
    --before ../data/baseline/2026-08-07 \
    --after  ../data/curated \
    --json /tmp/step2_diff.json --fail-on-delta
```

`--after` accepts `data/curated` directly; a snapshot is not required on both
sides.

### Sizing Step 2

`data/curated/solar_wind_daily.parquet`: 29,152 rows, 123 stations,
`sample_date` 2020-01-01 → 2026-08-06.

- Rows by year: 2020–2025 ≈ **2,555 each**; 2026 **13,808**.
- 2,555 = 7 stations × 365 days. **Only 7 of 123 stations have full 2020+
  history**; the other 116 have a median of **109 days**, all in 2026.
- So the gap is **temporal, not spatial** — nearly every location has *some*
  data; almost none has *history*.
- Of 671 window beaches: 584 have some coverage, **109 reach ≥95%**, 87 have
  none. Median per-beach coverage in the window is **11.3%**.
- The connector caches per (lat_0.1°, lon_0.1°) cell: **120 distinct cells for
  all 850 beaches, 107 for the 671 window beaches.** The backfill is ~120 fetch
  keys, not 850.
- Raw cache today: **11 MB across 439 files** for 7 full + 116 partial
  histories. Extrapolating the 7 completed cells to 107 gives roughly
  **150–200 MB** — worth measuring for real before wiring into the
  `hydro-…-v4` Actions cache, exactly as `REBUILD_PLAN.md` warns.

### ⚠️ The side-effect reconstruction has a 7-day blind window

`served_offset_weight` is first **logged** on **2026-07-29** (0 non-null rows on
2026-07-28, 1,812 on 2026-07-29). But the two-tier router went live around
**2026-07-22** (`CLAUDE.md`; merge `00612cae`). The column was added to
`_HISTORY_COLUMNS` by commit **`2a4e6d1c4`, dated 2026-07-29** — a week after the
router started serving.

So roughly **2026-07-22 → 2026-07-28 was served by the router but is recorded
as pre-router**, and every "pre-router vs router" split — including the one in
`REBUILD_PLAN.md` Phase 3 and this document's § 6 — **understates the router era
by about 7 days.** This is not a flaw in the reconstruction; it is proof that
side-effect reconstruction cannot work, which is precisely Step 8's argument.
The fingerprint must be written by the serving path at serve time, and Step 8
should treat the pre-2026-07-29 boundary as **unknowable**, not merely stale.

## 6. Bands and realized rates

**The band convention is left-closed** — `calibration.py::risk_band` is
`p < 0.20 → Low`, `p < 0.30 → Moderate`, `p < 0.70 → High`, else `Very High`.
This matters: the served isotonic has step plateaus that land *exactly* on
0.20 and 0.30, so using pandas' default right-closed `pd.cut` misassigns
**948 rows** (371 at exactly 0.20, 577 at exactly 0.30). Use `risk_band`.

Scored on the 14,414 serving-calibration fit pairs (last-issued forecast per
beach-day, outcome = same-day lab result else first in D+1..D+3), banding
`p_exceed`. CIs are **naive Wilson 95%**, treating rows as independent:

**CURRENT 0.20 / 0.30 / 0.70**

| band | n | realized | 95% CI (Wilson) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Low | 12,579 | 0.044 | [0.041, 0.048] |
| Moderate | 688 | 0.193 | [0.166, 0.224] |
| High | 1,060 | 0.240 | [0.215, 0.266] |
| Very High | 87 | 0.563 | [0.459, 0.663] |

**PROPOSED 0.10 / 0.20 / 0.45**

| band | n | realized | 95% CI (Wilson) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Low | 11,233 | 0.036 | [0.033, 0.040] |
| Moderate | 1,346 | 0.110 | [0.094, 0.128] |
| High | 1,549 | 0.216 | [0.196, 0.237] |
| Very High | 286 | 0.357 | [0.303, 0.414] |

**E2 fails under both schemes.** Moderate [0.166, 0.224] and High [0.215, 0.266]
overlap under CURRENT even with these *tighter-than-published* intervals; under
PROPOSED, Moderate and High separate but Low/Moderate nearly touch. The
`REBUILD_PLAN.md` conclusion stands; see § 8 for the CI-method discrepancy.

### The served band is not a pure function of `p_exceed`

Recomputing `risk_band(p_exceed)` and comparing to the logged `risk_band`
column: **14,407 of 14,414 match; 7 rows disagree** — all in the direction
`p_exceed` says High but the row was served Moderate. That is
`confidence_capped_risk_band` / the advisory-floor path. Small, but Step 10
must band on the *served* column, not on a recomputation, or it will grade 7
rows against a band the user never saw.

### The coverage discrepancy, resolved

The plan's 8.1% / 26.1% does **not** reproduce at 1095 days on today's data —
and no single window reproduces both the plan's `n` and its coverage figures.
Scanning window lengths on the production loader:

| window days | n | UV | wind/solar |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1080 | 87,591 | 9.07% | 26.88% |
| **1095** | **89,002** | **8.93%** | **26.72%** |
| 1160 | 94,901 | 8.38% | 26.10% ← plan's wind/solar |
| 1200 | 98,640 | 8.06% | 25.75% ← plan's UV |

Plan `n` = 88,319 implies ~1,090 days, where UV would be ~9.0%, not 8.1%. The
plan's own three numbers are mutually inconsistent under any window on today's
data.

**They are consistent on an older vintage.** The 2026-08-04 committed refresh
gives window n = 88,164, UV 7.96%, wind/solar 25.93% — and the 2026-08-05
refresh gives 88,959 / 8.77% / 26.57%. The plan's 88,319 / 8.1% / 26.1% sits
between them. **The plan's Phase 0 block was measured on a data vintage one to
two days older than this baseline, and the numbers were correct when written.**

The same explanation covers the assay-pair count (1,175 → 1,174 at 2026-08-04 →
1,172 today) and the window `n`. The San Diego share is the one place the plan
is genuinely off: no vintage measured gives 26.0% / 0.386.

**Operational consequence:** every coverage figure in this programme must be
quoted with its data vintage. Step 2's "≥95%" exit criterion has to be measured
against a pinned snapshot — `diff_curated.py --before data/baseline/2026-08-07`
— not against a number in prose.

### The CI discrepancy

The plan's band CIs are ~2–3× wider than naive Wilson (Low ±0.010 vs ±0.0035;
Very High ±0.20 vs ±0.10). That is the signature of a **cluster bootstrap over
beaches**, which is the more honest interval here — the same beach contributes
up to ~100 correlated rows, so naive Wilson overstates precision.

Both are recorded above because the E2 verdict is the same under either: the
Moderate and High intervals overlap under CURRENT even with the *tighter*
intervals. Step 10 should publish cluster-bootstrap CIs (the resampling unit is
the beach, matching `ml/evaluation.py`'s existing fold-level bootstrap), but the
conclusion does not depend on which is used.

## 9. Things found here that change Steps 2–10

1. **Step 2's scope is smaller than the plan implies, and better defined.** The
   backfill is ~**107–120 (lat_0.1°, lon_0.1°) cache keys**, not 850 beaches,
   and the gap is purely **temporal** — 116 of 123 locations already have 2026
   data and need 2020–2025. Estimated cache ~150–200 MB.
2. **Step 2's exit criterion needs a pinned baseline.** Coverage rises ~0.13
   pp/day from new rows alone, so "26.7% → ≥95%" measured against prose will
   drift. Diff against `data/baseline/2026-08-07`.
3. **Step 3 will not orphan any row.** The day-collapse key reproduces
   **492,543 / 492,543** matches against `observations.parquet` — the assay join
   is exact, so any unmatched row after Step 3 is a real bug, not expected loss.
4. **Step 8's premise is stronger than stated.** `served_offset_weight` was
   only added to the history log on **2026-07-29**, ~7 days after the router
   went live. The three eras cannot be reconstructed from side effects even in
   principle for 2026-07-22..28. Treat that span as **unknowable regime**, and
   do not "fix" it by back-dating — the fingerprint has to be written at serve
   time going forward.
5. **Step 10 must band on the served `risk_band` column.** 7 of 14,414 rows have
   a served band that `risk_band(p_exceed)` does not reproduce
   (confidence cap / advisory floor). Recomputing grades rows against a band no
   user saw.
6. **Step 10's band convention is left-closed.** Isotonic plateaus sit exactly
   on 0.20 and 0.30; a right-closed `pd.cut` misassigns 948 rows and silently
   changes every band count.
7. **`REBUILD_PLAN.md`'s San Diego share (26.0% / 57.6%, base 0.386) is wrong**
   and should be corrected to 24.7% / 57.0% / 0.405. It does not change any
   conclusion — the labelling-universe argument is if anything stronger at a
   4.03× base-rate gap.
