# Step 3 — assay identity in the label frame (2026-08-07)

Step 3 of `REBUILD_STEPS.md`. `build_beach_day_frame` now carries the assay of
the sample that won the same-day worst-sample collapse into `beach_day`:
`label_method`, `label_units`, `is_pcr`, and a per-beach-day
`assay_disagreement` flag.

**This step is schema-only. `data/curated/` was not regenerated** — the full
relabel is Step 4, deliberately separated because `cli.py:501-505` takes an
incremental path (last 7 days only) whenever `observations.parquet` exists and
no `--start-date` is passed, which is exactly what the daily workflow does.
Landing a label-shaped change through that path would relabel a week and leave
three years on the old definition, silently, passing every gate.

`diff_curated.py --before after-step2 --after after-step3` reports
**ZERO DELTAS across every category**.

## How these numbers were produced

Since no data was regenerated, every figure below is a **replay**: the shipped
`observations.parquet` is pushed through the new `build_beach_day_frame` and the
result joined onto the shipped `beach_day.parquet` on `(beach_id, sample_date)`.

| check | result |
|---|---|
| replay rows | **492,543** |
| shipped rows | **492,543** |
| duplicate `(beach_id, sample_date)` keys in the replay | **0** |
| joined rows matched | **492,543 / 492,543 = 100.0%** |
| `label_method` null after the join | **0** |
| `exceeds_stv` flips, replay vs shipped | **0** |
| `enterococcus_value` changes, replay vs shipped | **0** |

Step 1 predicted the 100% match; it reproduces exactly. The last two rows are
new and matter for Step 4 — see § 8.

Reproduce: `scratchpad/step3_replay.py` (~5 s, read-only).

## 1. What the column list gained

`beachwatch.py::_attach_assay_identity` runs on the ranked observation frame
before the collapse; the collapse's explicit column list then selects the four
new columns alongside the existing twelve.

| column | dtype | source |
|---|---|---|
| `label_method` | object | raw `method` of the winning sample |
| `label_units` | object | raw `units` of the winning sample |
| `is_pcr` | bool | `exceedance.is_pcr_measurement(method, units)` |
| `assay_disagreement` | bool | per beach-day, worst-of-each-class |

`is_pcr` is **delegated**, not re-implemented. That is load-bearing: it is the
same predicate `compute_exceeds_stv` uses to pick 1413 vs 104, so the column and
the label it describes cannot drift apart. `test_is_pcr_is_derived_via_the_
shared_exceedance_predicate` monkeypatches the predicate and asserts the output
follows it; replacing the call with `method == "ddPCR"` fails that test plus four
others (verified by mutation).

`method`/`units` absent is tolerated (the national WQP path supplies them as
null) and yields a culture-only frame rather than a `KeyError`.

## 4. Mixed-assay days and the worst-sample rule

A "mixed" beach-day carries at least one culture **and** at least one PCR
sample. Compared worst-of-each-class, matching how the label is built.

| | count | share |
|---|---|---|
| beach-days total | 492,543 | — |
| beach-days with any PCR sample | 18,789 | 3.81% |
| PCR-only beach-days | 17,617 | 3.58% |
| **mixed beach-days** | **1,172** | **0.238%** |
| (for scale) beach-days with >1 sample of any kind | 10,069 | 2.04% |

**All 1,172 mixed days are San Diego.** No other county runs both assays.

### The two rules agree half the time

| | value |
|---|---|
| agreement | **0.5043** |
| culture flags | 0.1220 |
| PCR flags | 0.6058 |
| PCR flags alone | **574 / 1,172 = 48.98%** |
| culture flags alone | **7 / 1,172 = 0.60%** |

Reproduces the baseline's "beach-day, worst-of-each" row exactly (1,172 pairs,
0.5043) and the brief's ~50.6% / 1,175 to within one data vintage.

### How often the worst-sample rule picks ddPCR on a mixed day

**1,164 of 1,172 = 99.32%.**

Split by whether the assays agreed:

| mixed day | n | won by PCR | share |
|---|---|---|---|
| assays **disagreed** | 581 | 574 | **98.80%** |
| assays **agreed** | 591 | 590 | **99.83%** |
| all | 1,172 | 1,164 | 99.32% |

Two distinct mechanisms, and both need stating:

1. **On disagreeing days the `exceeds_stv` rank decides**, and ddPCR flags at
   6× culture's rate, so it wins 574 of 581. The 7 exceptions are exactly the
   7 culture-flags-alone days.
2. **On agreeing days the *value* tiebreak decides — and it is comparing copies
   against MPN.** A copies count is numerically far larger than an MPN count for
   the same water (full-frame median: culture **10**, PCR **2,501**; p90 81 vs
   122,895), so the PCR row wins 590 of 591 *regardless of which reading is
   worse in its own units*. This is not the "worst sample" rule doing what its
   docstring says; on these days it is a units artefact. It changes no label
   (both classes agree, by definition) but it does decide which number lands in
   `enterococcus_value`.

Consequence (1) is the one the brief asked to quantify and it is real: **574
beach-days — all San Diego, across 20 beaches — are labelled positive only
because ddPCR won a mixed day.** Scoring the counterfactual "judge a mixed day
by its culture sample" against the shipped label:

| population | rows changed | 1→0 | 0→1 | positives |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| full frame | 574 | 574 | 0 | 51,042 → 50,468 (−1.12%) |
| raw 1095d window | 391 | 391 | 0 | 15,693 → 15,302 (−2.49%) |

All movement is one-directional. The 7 culture-flags-alone days are unaffected
(the collapse already gives them the culture label, since culture is the sample
that flagged). *The collapse rule is deliberately unchanged in this step;
`assay_disagreement` makes the exposure visible so Step 7 can price it.*

Consequence (2) is smaller in row count but wider in blast radius: it is one of
the ways `enterococcus_value` ends up carrying copies. See § 5.

## 6. Two data-quality findings surfaced by the new column

Both are pre-existing, neither changes any label today, and neither is fixed
here. They are recorded because `is_pcr` is the first thing that makes them
visible.

**(a) Three Mendocino rows are classified PCR.** `Enterolert` (a culture
method) with `Copies/100ml` units — almost certainly a unit-field data-entry
error at the source:

| beach | date | value |
|---|---|---|
| Caspar Headlands SB | 2024-10-08 | 10 |
| Pudding Creek Beach | 2025-05-20 | 10 |
| Hare Creek | 2025-10-28 | 20 |

`is_pcr_measurement` ORs on a `copies` unit, so `compute_exceeds_stv` **already**
judges these against 1413 rather than 104. All three values are ≤20, below both
thresholds, so no label moves. But the risk is live: a Mendocino Enterolert
reading between 104 and 1413 arriving with `Copies/100ml` units would be a
**silent false negative**, and after Step 4 these rows also make "PCR exists in
2 counties" true, which will contaminate any naive per-regime stratification in
Step 7. Do **not** narrow `is_pcr_measurement` to fix this — that would change
`compute_exceeds_stv`'s semantics for real San Diego rows. It belongs in
`pipeline/` as a source-data correction, alongside `county_corrections.py` /
`spelling.py`. **Recommended for Step 5.**

**(b) One `ddPCR` row is dated 2002-09-13** (San Diego, 375 copies, below the
BAV so no label impact). ddPCR was not operational in 2002; this is a source
date error. Harmless today, but it makes `min(sample_date | is_pcr)` a
misleading 2002 instead of 2022 — do not derive the PCR-era boundary from it.

## 7. Tests

`backend/tests/test_beach_day_assay.py` — 15 tests, all passing.

Single-assay identity (ddPCR → `is_pcr=True` + correct method/units; culture →
`is_pcr=False`); both single-signal PCR spellings (copies-units-only,
method-only); mixed days flagged on disagreement and **not** flagged when the
two agree (both-clean and both-exceed cases separately); `label_method`
reporting the sample that actually won the collapse, including the rare
culture-wins direction; disagreement scoped to the beach-day, not across
beaches; dtype/presence guard; missing `method`/`units` tolerated; the three
real PCR spellings parametrised; and the delegation guard.

Mutation-checked: replacing the `is_pcr_measurement` call with
`method.str.lower() == "ddpcr"` fails **5** of the 15, including the dedicated
delegation test.

Full backend suite: **586 passed** (571 before + 15 new). Ruff clean.

## 8. What this changes for Steps 4-10

1. **Step 4 is less risky than `REBUILD_STEPS.md` describes — but only on one
   of its two halves, and the split is worth exploiting.** Replaying
   `build_beach_day_frame` over the whole of the current `observations.parquet`
   reproduces the shipped `beach_day` **exactly**: 492,543/492,543 rows, **0
   `exceeds_stv` flips, 0 `enterococcus_value` changes**. So the incremental
   trap has *not* left the day-collapse half of the frame on a stale definition
   — `beach_day` is already consistent with the observations it was built from.
   Step 4's expected label movement therefore comes **entirely from
   re-normalising `observations.parquet` itself** from the raw sources, not from
   re-running the collapse.

   That makes Step 4's diff cleanly attributable if it is run in two stages:
   (i) rebuild `beach_day` from the *existing* `observations.parquet` — the diff
   must be **assay columns only, zero label flips**, and anything else is a bug;
   (ii) then re-normalise observations and rebuild again — all remaining movement
   is source-vintage. Run as one stage, the two are confounded exactly as the
   plan warns. **Recommended change to Step 4's method.**

2. **Step 4's "100% non-null `label_method`" exit criterion is reachable.**
   Zero nulls across all 492,543 rows, 27 distinct values.

3. **Step 7's leave-one-county-out is worse than "a different labelling
   universe" — it is a *within-county mixture*.** 62.3% of San Diego's window
   rows are PCR and 37.7% culture, spread over 43 beaches that carry both across
   time and 100 that carry only one. Holding out San Diego holds out ~all of one
   assay *and* a large culture cohort. Stratifying by `is_pcr` and by county are
   not the same cut, and neither substitutes for the other.

4. **San Diego's culture base rate is 0.0974 — statewide-normal.** Any narrative
   that treats San Diego as intrinsically the dirtiest county is an artefact of
   the pooled label. Worth stating in the Step 10 model card.

5. **Step 6's plume feature has a ready-made target variable.** `assay_disagreement`
   is a per-row, model-visible marker of exactly the Imperial-Beach→Buccaneer
   gradient Step 6 sets out to capture, on 581 rows over 20 beaches — enough to
   validate `dist_to_chronic_source_km` *before* spending a retrain on it.

6. **A mid-2022 label discontinuity now exists in the record.** San Diego goes
   0% → ~60% PCR-labelled between 2021 and 2023. Any window reaching before
   2022-05 mixes two label definitions on the same beaches — the same failure
   mode as Step 2's proxy-vs-real UV boundary at 2022-08-04, and the two
   boundaries are three months apart. A sample clean on both starts
   **2022-08-04**.

7. **Step 5 gains a concrete work item** (the Mendocino unit-field correction,
   § 6a) that is independent of, and cheaper than, the `curation.py` /
   `serving_repository.py` threshold cleanup already scoped there.
