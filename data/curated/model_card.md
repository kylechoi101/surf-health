# Model Card — Shorelife beach water-quality forecast

**Last substantive revision: 2026-08-08**, at the close of the 10-step
label/measurement rebuild (`REBUILD_STEPS.md`). Numeric metrics in this file are
regenerated by the daily run — read `data/curated/system_health.json` for live
values. The *findings* below are stable results, not snapshots.

---

## What this system does

Predicts, for ~650 California marine beach sampling stations, the probability
that today's water exceeds the enterococcus single-sample action value. Publishes
one `p_exceed` and one risk band per station per day.

**Serving configuration** is identified by `serving_config_fingerprint`
(`serving_path_version` 4, current fingerprint `6c28d645ca929f34`). Read that,
not `production_model`, to know what produced a number: `production_model` records
the registry *winner*, which is not necessarily the model that computed
`p_exceed`.

---

## Risk bands — these are RELATIVE risk tiers, not absolute probabilities

Cutpoints **0.10 / 0.20 / 0.70** (left-closed: `Low` is `p < 0.10`).

| band | realized rate | vs average day |
|---|---|---|
| Low | ~0.036 | ~0.5× |
| Moderate | ~0.110 | ~1.6× |
| High | ~0.216 | ~3× |
| Very High | ~0.563 | ~8× |

**A band labelled "Very High" delivers about 56 in 100 — it is high *relative to a
~7% base rate*, not a high probability in absolute terms.** Every band string
states its own multiplier and carries "this is a model estimate, not an official
advisory or lab result."

- The **Low/Moderate cut moved 0.20 → 0.10** on 2026-08-08, derived from
  `p_exceed_precal` over 11,000+ rows spanning every month.
- The **High/Very High cut (0.70) is PROVISIONAL.** It cannot be derived yet: 95%
  of the `p ≥ 0.45` evidence and 97% of the `p ≥ 0.70` evidence predates both the
  two-tier router and the persistence-pin removal. It becomes derivable once a
  single serving regime accumulates ~60 days (see *Regime stability*).

Two safety floors ride on these constants and both keep their invariants:
**persistence floor** = `_LOW_THRESHOLD` (a beach whose last sample exceeded is
never displayed Low); **advisory floor** = `_HIGH_THRESHOLD` (a beach with a
posted county advisory never renders Low or Moderate). Moving the cuts lowered
the displayed number on floored rows (0.20 → 0.10 and 0.30 → 0.20 respectively)
while preserving both guarantees.

## Irreducible limits — no amount of modelling work fixes these

1. **Labs sample weekly; the product answers daily.** Roughly **6 of 7**
   predictions are unverifiable *in principle*. This is the deepest constraint in
   the system, and every metric below is computed on the one day in seven that is
   not the hard case.
2. **The label is only 86.6% self-consistent.** On 4,155 same-station same-day
   split replicates, culture results disagree with themselves; the exceedance
   rate swings **0.049 → 0.183** on replicate choice alone, and the day-collapse
   deliberately takes the worst. **No model can score above that noise floor.**
3. **1413 vs 104 is a regulatory fact, not a modelling choice.** San Diego ddPCR
   is judged against 1413 copies/100 mL (CDPH-developed, EPA Region 9 approved);
   culture against the 104 MPN/CFU STV. It is the rule San Diego posts advisories
   on. Do not "fix" it.

## What the 2026-08 rebuild measured — stated unsoftened

### The label is two labels

`exceeds_stv` pools two assays that agree **50.6%** of the time on 1,175 paired
same-beach same-day samples. ddPCR is ~15.4% of rows but **~51.8% of all positive
labels** (base rate culture 0.100 vs ddPCR 0.592, **5.9×**).

**San Diego is not a dirtier coastline — it is a differently-labelled one.**
Culture-only base rate: San Diego **0.0974** vs rest-of-California **0.1007**.
Every leave-one-county-out result that held out San Diego was measuring a
labelling artifact, not spatial generalisation.

### The pooled metric concealed a 2.5× gap

| | n | base rate | AUCPR | **lift** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| culture | 10,536 | 0.0695 | **0.3875** | 5.58× |
| ddPCR | 1,919 | 0.6493 | **0.9707** | 1.50× |
| *pooled — previously published as "the" number* | 12,455 | — | *0.8168* | — |

The regime covering ~85% of California beaches scores **0.3875**. Because AUCPR
is base-rate dependent, *lift* inverts the ranking. **All metrics are now
stratified by assay; no blended figure is published as "the" number.**

Searcy operating points, stratified: culture **sens 0.650 @ spec 0.871** (which
beats the 0.50 @ 0.87 operational median from Searcy et al. 2018); ddPCR sens
0.857 @ spec 0.874.

### Three feature hypotheses failed

- **The marine/microbiology features are worth ~nothing.** Long claimed at
  "+0.029 AUCPR, spatially confirmed." Re-derived on complete data:
  **+0.0015, 95% CI [−0.0042, +0.0053]** held-out county; +0.0007 held-out beach;
  and **negative on culture rows** (−0.0053). The original figure was measured on
  data that was ~74% absent *and* 83% understated where present.
- **Photo-inactivation is null.** Within-beach culture-vs-UV looks strong at
  r = −0.378 [−0.404, −0.352] (n=86,837), but controlling season *and* rain —
  within beach-month cells, dry days only — it collapses to **r = −0.030
  [−0.070, +0.010]**, n=7,951. The apparent effect was the confounds.
- **`dist_to_chronic_source_km` is latitude in disguise.** Spearman **0.9968**
  with `latitude`; univariate AUROC of −distance is 0.863 on ddPCR rows but
  **0.479 — below chance** on statewide culture rows. It is retained as
  analyst-facing geometry and is **not** in the training feature set.

### The probabilities lose to a two-constant lookup table

The published "serving recalibration beats a flat base rate" claim compares
against a *single global* constant.

| baseline | Brier |
|---|---|
| model, pooled | 0.0970 |
| pooled flat constant | 0.1132 → model "wins" |
| **stratum-aware flat constant** | **0.0756 → model loses by ~28%** |

On **both** assay strata the served probabilities lose to a flat constant while
the pooled figure wins — Simpson's paradox on the assay mix.

### What did hold up

**The positive-persistence floor generalises, and more strongly outside San
Diego** than inside it — contradicting the previously-documented "overwhelmingly
San Diego ddPCR" caveat (764 of 2,119 affected rows are culture rows outside San
Diego):

| cut | n | Brier override | Brier floor | gap | 95% CI |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ALL | 2,119 | 0.2330 | 0.1171 | +0.1159 | [+0.0989, +0.1323] |
| ddPCR (San Diego) | 1,239 | 0.1784 | 0.0775 | +0.1009 | [+0.0636, +0.1237] |
| **culture, rest of CA** | 764 | 0.3142 | 0.1695 | **+0.1447** | **[+0.1181, +0.1760]** |

`is_pcr` also earns its place as a feature, and **not** as a San Diego proxy:
replacing it with an explicit San Diego indicator does *not* recover performance
(held-out beach within-group AUROC: base 0.8648, `no_is_pcr` 0.8519,
`sd_indicator` 0.8505). It encodes assay regime, which varies *within* San Diego
over time.

## Headline metric

**Within-beach AUROC in the served regime** — currently **0.7477** (culture
0.7235 / ddPCR 0.7585).

Global AUCPR is **demoted**: it is base-rate dependent and dominated by
*between-beach* variance, so a model can score well by knowing Tijuana Slough is
dirtier than Carmel with zero ability to tell Tuesday from Thursday. Measured
directly: diluting a fixed set of predictions moves AUCPR 0.532 → 0.322 with the
model and its ranking held constant, while AUROC stays flat.

## Regime stability and calibration honesty

The serving calibrator is fitted on a trailing 120-day window of served/lab
pairs. As of this revision that window is **86.3% pre-router, 6.9% unknowable,
6.9% router-era, 0.0% current configuration** — i.e. **the calibrator applied to
today's probabilities is fitted entirely on superseded configurations.**

This is reported, warned on, and **deliberately not fixed by filtering**:
filtering to the current regime leaves 0 pairs, the calibrator returns `None`,
and the product would serve uncalibrated probabilities, which are known to run
hot. Stale data beats no data until a clean window exists.

Winner churn was the cause: **11 winner changes in 105 days (~every 10 days)**
against ~7 days needed to calibrate one. A 60-day tenure floor plus a 7-run
confirmation streak now reduces that to **2 swaps over the same 105 days** in
replay. A clean single-regime calibrator becomes available ~6 days after a reset;
`live_fingerprint_fraction ≥ 50%` takes ~60 days.

**Known gap:** the 2026-07-22..07-28 span was router-served but is logged as
pre-router and is unknowable from side effects (`served_offset_weight` was only
added to the history schema on 07-29). It is bucketed as
`router_logging_gap_unknowable` rather than back-dated.
